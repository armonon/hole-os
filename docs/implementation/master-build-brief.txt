1. PRODUCT THESIS AND DECISIONS

Build a personal world for creating, learning, collecting, working, playing, and being with people. Creativity is the first complete use case; the underlying computer must support everyday life too.

Central interaction:
  Move closer to reveal what you can do with something.
  Move farther away to understand what it belongs to.

An object retains its identity through every view. A note becomes a readable preview and then an editable surface. Pulling back reveals its surrounding materials, project, Space, and place in the user's world. Positions, edits, provenance, and return paths persist.

Adopt three complementary product directions:

A. A personal place — the foundation.
   Someone returns to a song, school assignment, or trip and recognizes both the materials and the unfinished thought. Stable geography and trustworthy restoration provide the value.

B. An adaptive instrument — the everyday power.
   Selecting material reveals useful tools. Smart Screen can propose a temporary work surface for studying a PDF, comparing mixes, planning a journey, or making release artwork. Existing objects and running applications supply the work surface.

C. A network of rooms — the shared future.
   People invite others into a bounded project, listening session, class, or exhibition. Access, authorship, presence, and leaving are understandable.

Recommendation: build A as the durable foundation, B through shared capabilities and reversible arrangements, and C through real authorization and collaboration services. All three belong in the complete program. Their dependency order is not a reason to omit the later work.

Tradeoffs to make explicit:
- A asks us to invest heavily in reliable persistence, migration, and retrieval. Spatial arrangements can become cluttered, so direct search, outlines, and optional tidy views are essential.
- B can make tools easier to reach but can also make the interface unpredictable. Keep adaptation temporary or explicitly accepted, keep the source material visible, and let users pin a familiar arrangement.
- C makes the computer more social but introduces access boundaries, conflicts, and interruptions. Build scope and role visibility into the interface and make solitary work equally complete.
- Supporting several views increases maintenance and testing cost. Share services and instances, and ship fewer well-integrated interactions before expanding presentation variety.

Keep these product decisions consistent across agents:

- Flat Desktop, project canvas, Smart Screen, and optional Universe are views of one computer. They share assets, app instances, drafts, permissions, jobs, and project context.
- Flat Desktop is the dependable default. Users can choose another startup view. Universe is an optional presentation of real work, not a separate app or duplicate filesystem.
- The canvas host belongs to the OS. Luma can brand the creative canvas experience; other studio editors remain equal plug-ins.
- Spatial zoom changes context and information density. Time/history is a deliberate, separately labeled mode.
- Smart Screen proposes useful arrangements without silently moving the user's landmarks. Temporary views can be dismissed, saved, or explicitly applied with undo.
- People can open things directly through search, links, Recents, and keyboard navigation. They never have to fly through the world to perform a simple action.
- The computer remains useful without AI, an account, a network connection, or 3D graphics.
- Private is the default. Sharing a placement does not implicitly share every linked resource or surrounding Space.
- A smaller functional drawer is preferable to a larger drawer of promises. App count is not a completion metric.

3. EVERY FEATURE MUST DESCRIBE A HUMAN INTERACTION

For each proposal and implemented feature, record:
- What the person is trying to do in an ordinary moment.
- What action they take.
- What visibly changes on screen.
- What remains stable: resource identity, location, selection, draft, permission, return path.
- Why the interaction improves their life.
- What durable output or observable behavior proves it works.

Develop these extensions as real interactions:

Places that unfold:
  A song reveals recordings, lyrics, cover studies, and experiments. A trip reveals dates, places, reservations, plans, and photographs. Both share containers, references, history, tools, and return paths. Their useful previews differ: waveform/listening position for audio, map/day sequence for travel, reading position for study. Use typed project templates over common primitives, not unrelated navigation systems.

Tools around materials:
  Select a photograph; a small contextual strip offers crop, color, cutout when genuinely available, compare, and open in Luma. The photograph stays anchored. A focused editor may expand, but the surrounding project remains available. On phones, controls move into a reachable sheet rather than becoming tiny floating icons. Deep studio work remains available.

Connections across the world:
  Place one holiday photograph into a trip, palette study, and letter. Each placement references the same asset unless the user deliberately duplicates or branches it. Show “Used in…” and whether a placement follows the latest version or is pinned. A letter intended for sending pins an approved version. Traversing a connection preserves a return bookmark.

Places with memory:
  Returning restores the arrangement and working context. An optional “Where you left off” card points to the exact note, paragraph, playhead, or unresolved comparison. This is inspectable workspace state, not secret behavioral surveillance. Let people leave a short “next thought” beside an object. Memory can be corrected, excluded, exported, or forgotten.

Making places together:
  Invite a friend to compare two mixes. The invitation names the project and role, opens at the selected material, and displays the audience. Private notes stay outside the invitation. Show a guest preview before publishing a World. Provide leave, revoke, and ownership controls. Do not promise to retract copies already downloaded.

Personal instruments:
  Save a useful combination of references and tools as a reusable arrangement: a listening station, vocabulary practice surface, travel review, or poster export bench. Each control invokes a registered capability with inspectable inputs. The arrangement is editable and usable without conversational AI.

Permission to leave work unfinished:
  Spaces support piles, tentative branches, experiments, and quiet. No mandatory inbox zero, completion scoring, activity streak, or urgent notification system. Useful summaries should reduce the effort of returning, not impose a productivity ritual.

Confusing moments the design must resolve explicitly:
- “Did I open something or start typing?” Separate focus/preview from intentional editing.
- “Is this the same photograph?” Expose linked identity, pinned/current version, and independent duplication.
- “Where did my work go?” Keep stable landmarks, a dependable Back path, direct search, and return bookmarks.
- “Did that really save?” Show durable local state separately from pending sync and failures.
- “Why did everything move?” Preview adaptive arrangements and preserve a reversible prior arrangement.
- “Am I looking at now or yesterday?” Give History a persistent scope/date label and an obvious return to live work.
- “Who can see this?” Show audience at the Space/object boundary and preview the actual guest/public payload.
- “Why does my phone look different?” Adapt controls and layout while preserving identity, recognizable ordering, and the original desktop arrangement.
- “Why did an old result change?” Let people pin approved versions and inspect updates before adopting them.

4. THE FIRST COMPLETE JOURNEY AND STATE MACHINE

Implement this before expanding the interface across the whole app catalog:
  Your spaces → Studio → Night garden project → First thought note
  → edit → pull back to all spaces → leave/reload → return to the same thought.

The names are a clearly labeled test fixture or user-created project, never fake production data presented as the user's work.

States:
- WORLD: meaningful Space labels, stable placement, recognizable project summaries.
- SPACE: its project/cluster arrangements and relevant work become visible.
- PROJECT: real material references, project Kit, relationships, and tools.
- OBJECT_PREVIEW: readable content, version/save status, deliberate Edit action.
- OBJECT_EDIT: accessible editor, selection/caret or media position, relevant tools.
- HISTORY: explicit read-only historical view with scope and date/version labels.
- NAVIGATE: explicit optional spatial camera control, separate from text input.

Represent presentation mode separately from navigation scope. Smart Screen/Desktop/Universe must not be competing copies of WORLD/SPACE/PROJECT data. Represent editor state, history state, active project, focus, and navigation mode explicitly rather than inferring them from CSS scale.

Transitions:
1. Enter Studio by click/tap, keyboard activation, direct link, or intentional semantic zoom. Anchor the transition on the selected Space. Save the departure camera.
2. Open Night garden from its stable position. Reveal its real materials rather than replacing the entire scene with an unrelated page.
3. Focus First thought. Its card becomes readable. Zoom alone does not start editing.
4. Choose Edit. Route keyboard input to the editor. Persist a durable draft against a known base version. Report saving, saved locally, syncing if applicable, or failure truthfully.
5. Keep durable drafts continuously during editing, then checkpoint grouped edits as a new immutable version when the user chooses Done or leaves editing. Avoid creating a permanent version for each keystroke. A failed checkpoint retains the draft and its visible pending/error state. “Saved” means the relevant durable transaction succeeded; label “Draft saved” distinctly from a committed version.
6. Pull back. Preserve the same asset ID, arrangement, selection, and parent camera. If save fails, retain the draft and offer retry/export; do not imply success or discard the text.
7. Return to all spaces through breadcrumbs, Back, overview control, or zoom. Do not require the exact reverse gesture sequence.
8. Reload or reopen after an acknowledged durable save. Reconstruct the project, resource, arrangement, relevant editor state, and a clear route back. Do not depend on beforeunload to make drafts durable.
9. Reenter the project. The user finds the edited note and its surrounding materials without reconstructing context. Restore caret/scroll when appropriate without unexpectedly stealing focus.

Navigation requirements:
- Use separate enter/exit thresholds or another tested hysteresis policy to prevent oscillation at zoom boundaries.
- Preserve the selected anchor through transitions. Handle deletion, missing resources, revoked access, and stale links with a labeled destination and route home.
- Breadcrumb path describes the current visit path; ownership/location details describe the underlying resource. A linked object can have several visit paths without changing identity.
- Browser Back/Forward, explicit Back, parent navigation, search jumps, and connection jumps must have documented, consistent behavior.
- Separate object dragging from camera movement using tested gesture thresholds and cancellation. Never interpret a cancelled drag as an edit or drop.
- Persist meaningful transition endpoints and settled geometry. Avoid writing every animation frame.

6. SHARED DOMAIN MODEL AND SERVICE CONTRACTS

Publish small, versioned contracts before parallel implementation. Use the repository's language and module conventions; JSON schemas/JSDoc are sufficient if the project remains JavaScript. Avoid a gratuitous infrastructure rewrite.

Core entities:
- Account: authenticated identity when the real service is available; local identity remains distinct.
- Device: synchronization identity and device-specific presentation/capability information.
- Space: a place and its access boundary; not a synonym for every project or folder.
- Project: durable work membership, name, Kit, release packages, and relationships.
- Asset: stable identity, kind, display name, current head/version references, revision, lifecycle state.
- AssetVersion: immutable content reference, MIME/size/hash where applicable, parent version IDs, operation, parameters, provenance, author/job/time.
- ProjectMembership: explicit association between a project and resource; moving a visual card does not change it implicitly.
- Placement/SpatialObject: instance ID, scene ID, resource reference, follow-latest or pinned-version policy, geometry, presentation, accessibility label.
- SpatialScene: placements, layout schema version, named camera bookmarks, rendering preferences.
- Draft: editor instance, resource/base version, durable content or delta, revision, update time, recovery status.
- WorkspaceSnapshot: arrangement, app instances, resource references, selection, cameras, device layout class, and schema version.
- ActivityJob: verb/version, immutable input versions, parameters, state, progress, outputs, retry/cancellation data, idempotency key.
- ConnectionGrant: app identity/origin, resources/projects, allowed operations, expiry/revocation, originating user intent.
- SharePermission: subject, scope, role, conditions, server revision, revocation.
- AppCapability/Verb: supported inputs, parameters, requirements, permission needs, execution and output contracts.
- FlowRun: recipe version, concrete inputs, steps, jobs, outputs, restart/retry state.
- PublishTarget/ReleasePackage: destination or export type, explicitly selected version manifest, preview, publication state.
- MemoryEntry: optional explicit or inspectable retained context, source, scope, retention, deletion/export behavior; distinct from raw chat and application state.

Containment rule: a Space contains placements of projects and loose objects. A Project owns work membership and can appear in multiple Spaces. A Cluster groups placements without changing project membership or resource ownership. Preserve existing cluster identity/nesting through adapters; the navigation path is not a filesystem ownership tree.

Identity rules:
- Multiple placements can reference one asset. Removing a placement does not delete the asset.
- “Duplicate” creates an independent asset; “Link here” creates another placement; “Branch” creates a new asset with provenance from an existing version. Label these distinctly.
- Restoring an old version creates a new current version; history remains intact.
- One-to-many transforms such as stem splitting create derived assets linked to input versions. Do not make several unrelated stems successive versions of one original song.
- Reading a pinned version does not silently update it. Follow-latest placements update only after durable commits and indicate relevant changes without disruptive motion.
- Opening a pinned version for modification offers Edit latest or Branch this version. The approved pinned placement remains unchanged unless the user explicitly repins it. Saving through another placement never silently advances it.
- Sharing and ownership never derive solely from where a card is drawn.
- Sharing must explicitly authorize a pinned version or an approved stream of future versions. A follow-latest placement cannot silently expose a private branch, later unapproved content, or new dependencies. Enforce grants for each requested version, preview, and derivative.

Services:
1. ResourceService: import/read/draft/commit/branch/trash/restore/export with revision checks.
2. WorkspaceService: spaces, placements, scenes, snapshots, navigation bookmarks, layouts.
3. AppRuntime: instance IDs and open/focus/serialize/restore/suspend/close lifecycle.
4. CapabilityRegistry: typed actions and availability, routed consistently from every surface.
5. JobService: durable state machine and atomic result publication.
6. EditorRegistry: choose in-place editors by supported resource types and capabilities.
7. HoleBus: typed revisioned events emitted after successful commits, not a second source of truth.
8. BridgeService: connection/grant/message lifecycle for integrated applications.
9. SearchService: permission-aware local index of resources, projects, places, commands, and relevant history.
10. Sync/CollaborationService: real remote authorization and state reconciliation behind explicit availability.
11. AgentService: plans and executes registered capabilities under the same grants as manual operations.

Specify conflict and error results as part of these contracts. A stale revision must not silently replace a newer edit. App/verb schemas need version negotiation and tested compatibility behavior.

7. PERSISTENCE, MIGRATION, RECOVERY, AND TRUST

Use the existing IDB implementation as the first durable backend and evolve it incrementally. Move critical workspace state toward revision-aware transactions through adapters; preserve legacy loading and setup imports while compatibility is required.

Before changing schemas:
- Implement and test a full private backup/export format distinct from portable Setup.
- Include file bytes, metadata, lineage, projects, drafts, placements, arrangements, and necessary preferences in a versioned manifest with integrity checks.
- Exclude live authentication tokens and secrets from ordinary exports. Describe sensitive export contents clearly.
- Restore into a separate empty store/profile in tests and verify content plus references.
- Treat imported archives as untrusted: validate schema, manifest, expanded byte limits, integrity hashes, references, and safe archive paths. Stage restoration separately; never auto-execute imported content. Malformed/truncated archives and quota failure must leave the existing library intact.

Migration:
- Preserve original IDs and raw legacy snapshots. Maintain a mapping when a new entity structure is unavoidable.
- Use additive changes, an idempotent journal, validation, and an explicit cutover point.
- Retain orphaned or ambiguous lineage honestly rather than inventing parents or ownership.
- Keep unknown compatible fields when round-tripping formats where feasible.
- Coordinate open tabs and schema upgrades; use revisions/compare-and-swap semantics or a proven equivalent for writes.
- Verify interruption before, during, and after cutover. Recovery must resume or safely retain the previous state.
- Enforce reader/writer schema compatibility so an older build cannot corrupt a newer store. Rollback after cutover must preserve subsequently acknowledged edits through a compatible reader, conversion, or journal replay; restoring only a pre-migration snapshot would lose new work.
- Do not delete old stores or archived modules until replacement and restore gates pass.
- Do not relabel Setup v1/v2 as a complete backup.

Transaction rules:
- Commit file bytes, versions, relevant membership, and successful job outputs consistently. For operations spanning nontransactional systems, use a durable staged manifest and recovery protocol; do not claim cross-service atomicity.
- Jobs finish only after outputs are durable. Retries use idempotency keys and do not silently create duplicate results.
- Store crash-recoverable drafts during editing, with visible pending/error states. Only acknowledged durable content is guaranteed restored.
- Trash is recoverable. Permanent purge is distinct, explains dependencies, and follows explicit intent. Garbage collection must respect pinned versions, histories, shares, and backups/retention policies.
- A cancelled job has a defined result policy. Either nothing is published, or already committed partial outputs are explicitly identified and recoverable.
- Quota, unavailable storage, permission denial, corrupt import, and offline errors preserve originals and offer a usable next action.

Distinguish Saved locally, Sync pending, Synced, Conflict, and Failed. Browser storage is not equivalent to an independent backup. Provide storage usage, export, and recovery affordances without alarming or misleading language.

Replace app-name singleton assumptions with stable app instance IDs where needed. Opening the same studio on different resources must not silently retarget or overwrite another editor. Reuse an instance only through an explicit, safe policy.

Mode changes must preserve running instances and in-memory state. Keep ordinary DOM editors at a stable runtime location where possible; changing a camera or view must not remount an iframe/editor and lose work. If a constrained platform requires suspension, checkpoint and restore through the app contract and make limitations visible.

Canvas editor sequence:
- Text and image first, using real resources and durable versions.
- Audio trim/loop/level and playback next using validated browser audio support.
- Video preview/trim/captions, PDFs, 3D previews, and specialist studio bridges next.
- “Open in studio” must open the current resource/version with project context and a dependable route back.
- Shared canvas objects remain references; the host must not become the owner of every specialist file format.

Every integrated studio must:
1. Open a specified asset/version ID through an authorized resource handle.
2. Save back a new version or declared derived assets and receive committed IDs.
3. Read the permitted project Kit: fonts, colors, logo, and related settings.
4. Report long-running work through Activity with real states.
5. Expose at least one genuine registered verb or clearly declared read-only capability where the product is a reader/player.
6. Serialize/restore relevant editor state and report unsupported capabilities honestly.

Bridge protocol:
- Versioned handshake with exact approved origin, source window/channel, nonce/session ID, request ID, operation, and schema validation.
- Grants scoped to named resources/projects and actions, with expiry and revocation.
- No wildcard targetOrigin for sensitive messages; validate incoming origin and source. Never send session/account tokens or the entire private desktop to an arbitrary app.
- Bound payload sizes and define Blob/ArrayBuffer transfer, errors, cancellation, and cleanup.
- Bind asynchronous results to the original project, input versions, and base revision. Switching the active project while a job runs must not redirect its output.
- Acknowledge save only after durable commit. Repeated messages must be idempotent.
- Test unknown origin, wrong source, malformed message, stale grant, revoked access, duplicate request, and conflicting base version.

Untrusted executable content needs a real isolation boundary. Third-party apps, generated tools, and imported active content must not inherit the OS origin's ambient storage privileges. Use isolated origins or correctly sandboxed execution surfaces and brokered capabilities. Validate the transport model for sandboxed/opaque origins rather than weakening origin checks. Test that an installed/generated app cannot directly read the workspace database or bypass a revoked grant; postMessage validation alone cannot restrict arbitrary same-origin JavaScript.

The audited build rejects literal iframe markup, and current CSP/Permissions-Policy are restrictive. Inspect and deliberately update policies only for required capabilities. Do not remove protections globally to make embedding work. A target site's own framing policy may prevent embeds. Support real connected-window integration where possible, and clearly label external handoff when no bridge exists. Do not bypass framing restrictions or describe download/reupload as automatic integration.

9. VERBS, CAPTURE, CARRY, TIME, FLOW, AND PUBLISH

Verb contract:
  id, version, label, kind(transform|command), accepted input types/cardinality,
  parameter schema, capability requirements, permission scope, output schema,
  progress, cancellation/retry policy, and execution handler.

Handlers receive scoped immutable input versions, parameters, abort signal, and progress reporting. They return proposed outputs or metadata operations. The host performs authorized durable commits. AI, context menus, Quick Tools, Carry, canvas, and Flow invoke the same registry.

Do not force every action to create a content version. “Send to Carry” and “Add to project” are reversible metadata commands. Crop, convert, and text editing create content versions; split/extract may create derived assets. Record the actual operation in Activity/history.

Implement starter actions in a capability matrix:
- Text save/branch, image crop/resize/thumbnail, PNG/WebP conversion: begin with known real primitives.
- Cutout: integrate the actual Luma capability after inspecting engine and processing requirements.
- Audio trim/normalize/convert/extract and video thumbnail/caption operations: qualify codec support and processing costs; provide real outputs and fallbacks.
- Key/BPM and stem separation: locate and validate actual engines/models or implement a genuine supported path. Missing models or native-only engines are not working web verbs.
- Compress must declare its actual codec/quality/size behavior; renaming an extension is never conversion.

Capture:
- Quick text, links, user-pasted images, dropped files, and voice memo where supported.
- Show the destination: active project or Inbox. Preserve creation time and source metadata.
- Mic access follows a user action and usable permission state. Current deployed microphone policy must be fixed deliberately before recording can work. Test denied/unavailable/device-loss cases.
- Do not continuously read clipboard or record ambient audio.

Carry:
- Persistent references and snippets in a small shelf available across views.
- Preview/play where appropriate; drag or choose an explicit destination.
- Changing projects does not silently reassign a carried item. Indicate current destination.
- Work on keyboard/touch as well as drag/drop. Respect missing and revoked resources.

Time:
- Explicit asset/project/arrangement history with scope labels.
- Text diff, image comparison, and audio A/B where supported.
- Restore and Branch preserve the record; historical view is read-only until deliberate action.
- Workspace arrangement restore must define whether it affects positions, windows, and active resources; it must not unexpectedly roll content back.

Flow:
- Durable versioned recipes composed of registered actions.
- Preview concrete inputs, parameters, expected outputs, and required capabilities before consequential execution.

12. UNIVERSE, INPUT, ACCESSIBILITY, AND PERFORMANCE

Scene positions, depth, rotation, scale, anchors, and cameras are presentation data. The existing camera field z means zoom; do not silently reinterpret it as spatial depth. Migrate or version representations explicitly.

Input policy:
- Scene navigation owns gestures on the scene surface. Active editors own their content gestures. Breadcrumbs and an explicit Further out control remain available to leave editing; scrolling or magnifying editor content must never unexpectedly change navigation scope.
- Normal desktop wheel/trackpad behavior remains predictable. Do not globally steal document scrolling.
- Semantic zoom can use established gestures plus explicit controls; browser page magnification remains available.
- WASD/QE and faster movement apply only in an explicit Explore mode with no editable focus.
- F may focus selection only in an appropriate navigation context.
- Escape leaves pointer lock/navigation before other scope changes and never discards a draft.
- Tab and Shift+Tab remain native focus traversal. Replace the earlier proposed Tab-to-overview shortcut with a dedicated control or nonconflicting configurable shortcut.
- Cmd/Ctrl+K can open command/search if it does not intercept a more specific active editor behavior; provide a visible alternative.
- Pointer lock is optional, user-initiated, and visibly escapable. Basic navigation never requires it.
- Touch supports explicit enter/back/focus and reachable controls. Drag-only operations have alternatives.

Accessibility:
- Use semantic DOM controls and a logical reading/focus order in every presentation.
- Offer a list/tree equivalent to spatial discovery and search that reaches every permitted object.
- Focus indicators, labels, announcements, dialogs, contrast, and state errors must be usable with assistive technology.
- Edit text at a readable size rather than scaling it into a texture. Support browser zoom and reflow; 320px width must retain reachable essential controls.
- Reduced motion removes camera travel/parallax and uses immediate or restrained transitions while preserving orientation.
- Keyboard-only use must complete the entire first journey and core file workflow.

Device adaptation:
- Preserve conceptual object identity and geography. A phone can show an ordered outline or focused panel of the same scene.
- Keep device-specific presentation overlays separate from the canonical arrangement; visiting on a phone must not overwrite the user's desktop layout.
- Mark local-only unavailable assets and offer download/reconnect paths. Do not draw a convincing preview of inaccessible content.

Rendering/performance:
- Choose DOM/CSS and optional lazy-loaded WebGL after measuring the current stack. WebGPU is a progressive enhancement.
- Keep live editable DOM accessible. Do not make a renderer with browser-zoom limitations the only editing path.
- Bound visible scene complexity, texture resolution, pixel ratio, and active previews. Virtualize large collections.
- Pause hidden rendering and inactive previews without stopping intentionally playing audio. Revoke object URLs and release media/GPU resources when safe.
- Recover from graphics context loss and unsupported features by preserving work and falling back to Flat.
- Establish measured budgets on named reference devices before scaling. Suggested initial targets: responsive editing with 200 indexed assets, smooth navigation around 30 visible objects, and no lost input while background jobs run. Record real frame/input/memory measurements rather than claiming universal 60fps.
- Test relevant Safari and Chromium versions; do not claim a browser was verified if its runtime was unavailable.

17. HISTORY AND ORIGINALITY

Credit the lineage accurately. Hole did not invent hypertext, linked objects, collaboration, contextual tools, zoomable interfaces, or calm technology.

- Engelbart and his team's 1968 NLS demonstration combined linked information and collaborative work: https://www.dougengelbart.org/mousesite/1968Demo.html
- Bederson, Hollan, and collaborators explored multiscale/zoomable graphical interfaces in Pad++: https://chi1995.acm.org/proceedings/shortppr/bbb_bdy.htm
- Weiser and Brown described technology that works with the center and periphery of attention: https://calmtech.com/papers/coming-age-calm-technology

Hole's proposed extension is a coherent practical combination: durable identity across tools and views, meaningful zoom into real editable work, explicit recoverable memory, adaptable instruments, and understandable sharing. Treat its usefulness as a hypothesis to test through ordinary journeys, not a historical first to proclaim.
